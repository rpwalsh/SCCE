# T12 — Which cognitive operators buy information, and at what cost

Measured from `.scce/traces/*.jsonl`: 290 files, 216,052 events, **2,828 completed turns**, 28,588 s of turn wall
clock. Tool: `tools/operator-roi.mjs` — read-only, derives everything from events already on disk. No production
code changed, no operator altered, the server never touched.

Reproduce: `node --max-old-space-size=4096 tools/operator-roi.mjs --traces=<dir>`

## The proof-uncertainty definition, and what is wrong with it

**H = log2(|surviving hypothesis set|), in bits. An operator's contribution is dH = log2(N_before) - log2(N_after),
read from the counters that operator already writes into its own trace event.**

Chosen because it is the only quantity every operator already records. `graph.resolve.anchor_admissibility` writes
`{candidates, promoted, admitted}`; `graph.resolve.pool_admission` writes `{pool, admitted}`; `proof.entailment`
writes `{evidence, certifiedEvidence}`; `turn.support_candidates` writes `{admissible, ranked, candidates}`;
`planner.select` writes `rejected[]`. Nothing is inferred and nothing is simulated.

Alternatives rejected, with reasons:
- **Score entropy** over `proof.support_assessment.weights` / `proof.path_semiring` support masses is a truer
  posterior, but exists at exactly one point in the turn, so it yields no before/after for any operator. The tool
  computes it where present, as a cross-check only.
- **Unresolved proof obligations** (`proof.semantic.counts.obligations`) is recorded before but never after.
- **Contradiction mass** (`proof.path_semiring`, `contradiction.check`) is a scalar with no paired reading.

Weaknesses, stated plainly:
1. It assumes a **uniform posterior** over survivors. A pruner that discards the right answer scores exactly the
   same as one that keeps it. dH measures *narrowing*, not *correctness*. `basis%` is the separate, independent
   check on whether the narrowing was any good.
2. It gives **no credit for score sharpening** without a cardinality change. An operator that reorders a set
   without shrinking it reads as 0 bits. `mouth.*` and `candidate.cognitive.plan` plausibly do exactly this, which
   is why they read `n/a` rather than 0.
3. Counters are **per-operator vocabularies**, not one shared set. `candidates`→`admitted` and `pool`→`admitted`
   are not the same population, so bits are comparable *within* an operator over time and only roughly across
   operators.

**CPU attribution.** Turns run at 1.033 mean CPU-ms per wall-ms (`resourceUsage.cpuUserMs + cpuSystemMs` over
`wallClockMs`, n=2828), with `diskBytesRead: 0` — essentially fully CPU-bound, so wall-share apportionment is
defensible. An operator's CPU is its own `durationMs` scaled by its turn's CPU/wall ratio. Sound **only** for
operators that emit `durationMs`; 20 of 40 do not and are reported at zero cost rather than a guessed one.

**A measurement defect worth recording, because it inverted the headline.** Keying operators on `stage` alone is
wrong: `graph.resolve` is **five different operators sharing one stage**, separated only by `label`. Collapsed,
it read as a single 11,047-invocation, 9,725 CPU-s monster. Split by label it is a 3.4 s/turn graph slice, a 47 ms
semantic retrieval, and a **5.4 ms PowerWalk** — with opposite conclusions for each. The first version of this
finding also claimed PowerWalk was absent from the traces; that claim was false and is retracted below.

## What the numbers say

**1. The ablation result is explained, and not in the direction the ablation implied.** PowerWalk runs on
essentially every turn (2,759 invocations over 2,665 turns) and costs **5.4 ms, 0.0% of CPU, 16.7 CPU-seconds
across 2,828 turns**. Removing it cannot hurt an aggregate benchmark because it is 0.05% of a 10-second turn, and
it cannot help one either. "Removing PowerWalk does not hurt the benchmark" was never evidence that PowerWalk is
useless — it is evidence that the benchmark cannot resolve a 5 ms operator. **The ablation was measuring nothing.**

**2. The best-ROI operator in the system is a graph operator, by 10x.** `graph.semantic_retrieval` removes
**1.842 bits** per invocation, changes the hypothesis set in **95%** of invocations, and costs **47 ms** —
**37.85 bits/CPU-second**. Nothing else is close. It is 0.4% of attributable CPU. The cognitive machinery does buy
information; it buys it in the cheap operators.

**3. The expensive operators are the ones that buy nothing measurable.** The top three rows are 76.6% of all
attributable CPU. Of the one among them that is instrumented for uncertainty,
`graph.anchor_evidence_search` — 8,096 CPU-seconds, 3.7 s per invocation — left the gathered set **identical in
100% of 2,126 instrumented invocations**, mean 0.002 bits, **ROI 0.00**. Its prose filter is, by its own counters,
a no-op on the hypothesis set. `graph.slice` (9,653 CPU-s, 3.4 s/invocation, 27.8% of CPU) and `language.hydrate`
(2,295 CPU-s, **16.2 s per invocation**) record no before/after at all.

**4. The proof lane is the worst ROI in the system: 0.012 bits/s, ~300x below planning.** `proof.entailment`
(668 CPU-s) changes `evidence`→`certifiedEvidence` in 2% of invocations. `contradiction.check` and
`candidate.proposal.admit` are both 98% no-change, and `candidate.proposal.admit` removes **0.000 bits**. The proof
machinery almost never rejects anything it is handed. That is consistent with known-bug #4 — an apparatus with no
negative signal cannot produce one — and it is the mechanism behind known-bug #2 (9 of 40 declined when
answerable): the proof lane is not filtering, so whatever declines is decided elsewhere.

**5. Never decisive, by their own counters (sound negatives).**
- `graph.anchor_evidence_search` — 100% no-change over 2,126 invocations, for 23.3% of system CPU.
- `candidate.selected_evidence` — 100% no-change, 0.000 bits; it re-emits `ranked` as `spans` unchanged.
- `candidate.proposal.admit` — 98% no-change, 0.000 bits.
- `contradiction.check`, `proof.entailment` — 98% no-change; decisive on at most 1 turn in 50.

**6. Genuinely buys information.** `graph.anchor_admissibility` removes **3.966 bits** per invocation (32
candidates → 9 admitted, 1% no-change) and supplies most of graphSlice's 14,642 bits — but emits no `durationMs`,
so its cost is unknown. `graph.semantic_retrieval` (1.842 bits, ROI 37.85). `turn.support_candidates` (1.178 bits,
45% no-change). `planner.select` (0.576 bits, 49% no-change) at the best lane ROI, 1.6% of turn wall clock.

**7. High `basis%` is not good news.** `graph.pool_admission` retains the final claim basis in 92% of turns and
`candidate.score` in 96% — the answer's evidence was already present and survived. High `basis%` *with* `nochg%`
at 61-100% is the signature of a filter that passes everything through, not of one that finds something.

**8. Cost is not where cognition is.** 47% of turn wall is graphSlice and 26.4% is `language.hydrate` seeding
(16.2 s per invocation, zero instrumented bits). This directly contradicts the sentence on the wall: knowledge is
being loaded because it exists.

## What could not be instrumented, and why

- **PowerWalk is reachable and measured** (see above). **Query diffusion and relation potential are not.** Neither
  `diffusion` nor `relation_potential`/`relationPotential` appears as a stage, label or counter in any of the 290
  trace files covering these 2,828 turns; the only related counter is `powerWalkExpandedSeeds` on
  `graph.resolve|kernel.turn`. Per the skeptic clause these two are reported **unreachable from a real turn** —
  not cheap, not expensive, and not shown to run. Whether they are off the production path or merely untraced
  needs a call-path audit this task was scoped out of.
- **Per-operator CPU for 20 of 40 operators** — they emit no `durationMs`. This includes
  `graph.anchor_admissibility`, the single largest information producer measured. One line per `kernelTrace` call
  site would fix it, but that is a production change and out of scope.
- **True decisiveness** (would the answer differ without this operator?) is not derivable from traces of runs where
  the operator always ran. `nochg%` is the sound negative substitute; `basis%` the upper bound. A real number needs
  a per-operator bypass, which is scheduler-adjacent and explicitly out of scope.
- **Answer-changed** could not be attributed per operator, for the same reason.
- **`seedMs` bits.** `language.hydrate` records `importedUnits`/`constructionBundles` but no before/after pair, so
  its 7,534 s buys an unmeasurable amount.
- **Bytes hydrated** is recorded only on `graph.resolve|kernel.hot_neighborhood`, which fires 38 times inside turn
  windows (most of its ~3 GB of hydration happens outside `turn.input`→`turn.output`, during warmup). Per-turn
  bytes hydrated is therefore **not measurable** from the current trace; the `MB_hyd` column is near-empty by fact,
  not by omission.

## Honest limits

- The 2,828 turns are whatever traffic produced these 290 trace files; they are **not** a controlled task
  distribution. Per the north star, the right benchmark is tasks retrieval cannot solve. This traffic is dominated
  by exactly the easy retrieval the north star predicts will hide operator value. **A 0-bit reading here means
  "buys nothing on this traffic", not "buys nothing in principle."** That distinction is why this table is not yet
  grounds for deleting anything.
- `nochg%` is sound in one direction only: 100% no-change proves the operator could not have mattered on those
  turns; it does not prove it is useless on harder ones.
- CPU apportionment by wall share is justified by the 1.033 CPU/wall ratio but is still an apportionment.
- The counter pairs in `OPERATORS` are my reading of each operator's own vocabulary. Where an operator's
  `before`/`after` keys name different populations the bits are wrong; I removed one such pair
  (`candidate.cognitive.plan`, `proposals`→`activeOperators`) after it produced negative bits, and there may be
  others I did not catch.

## What a later task should do with this

The ROI ranking says the scheduler question is not "should PowerWalk run" — PowerWalk is 5 ms and the ablation
that condemned it could not see it. The question is **why 47% of wall clock goes to a graph lane whose productive
operators (`anchor_admissibility` at 3.97 bits, `semantic_retrieval` at 37.85 bits/s) are not the ones spending
the time**, and why `language.hydrate` spends 26% of the turn for no measured narrowing. Before any scheduler is
built: give the 20 uninstrumented operators a `durationMs`, and build the hard-task benchmark the north star calls
for — otherwise a scheduler will be fitted to traffic that does not need one.
