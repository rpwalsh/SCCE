# T4 — calibration fitting (promise G7)

Claim under test: SCCE fits its own decision weights from outcomes.

Result: **1 of 56 declared calibration ids can honestly be fitted from `calibration_observations`.**
54 have zero observations and cannot be fitted at all. The 55th (`candidate.mass`) has 3,321
observations and still fails, because its fit does not survive a change of split.

Tool: `tools/fit-calibration-from-observations.mjs` — offline, read-only SQL, no server.
Results file: `artifacts/calibration/observation-fit.json` (under the gitignored `/artifacts/`,
the same place `tools/fit-ranking-weights.mjs` writes; fitted values are not version-controlled).

## Held-out method

Source-disjoint split by `source_record_id`, produced by the kernel's own
`fitAndEvaluateCalibrationObservations` (holdout fraction 0.25, seeded, split hash recorded).
No source group appears in both halves. Two baselines, because one of them is a trap:

- **bootstrap** — the shipped value (the raw score, or the bootstrap coefficients).
- **base rate** — predict the fit split's positive rate for everything. A fit that loses to a
  constant is not useful no matter how much it beats bootstrap.

A fit is `improved` only if it beats **both**, by more than the paired standard error of the
per-observation difference. AUROC is reported alongside so calibration gains and discrimination
gains can be told apart.

## Fitted, and refused

**Fitted — `judge.requirement_weights`, probability calibration.** Beats the raw score by
0.047 Brier and a constant predictor by 0.041 +- 0.004, on 5 of 5 splits. AUROC 0.66-0.72 ->
0.68-0.77. This is the one id with honest held-out evidence.

**Refused — `candidate.mass`.** 3,321 observations, and the fit still fails. It beats its raw
score hugely (0.313 -> 0.195 Brier) but that only says the raw mass was never a probability: it
loses to a constant predictor on 4 of 5 splits, and the sign of the gain flips with the split.
The underlying reason is visible in raw AUROC across splits — 0.622, 0.576, 0.498, 0.353, 0.557.
At seedC the recorded mass is *anti*-correlated with the recorded outcome. There is no stable
relation here to fit. Reported as no-improvement; nothing installed.

**Refused — `judge.requirement_weights` coefficients.** The softmax refit beats bootstrap on
held-out Brier on every split, but by 0.001 (paired se 0.00007), and it loses to a constant on 4
of 5. What it does do consistently is improve *ranking*: AUROC rises on 5 of 5 splits, by +0.042,
+0.035, +0.023, +0.039, +0.049. Since judge.ts consumes these weights to order candidates rather
than as a probability, that gain is the operative one and it is real — but it is a discrimination
result, not a calibration one, and the tool labels it `improved_discrimination_only` rather than
counting it as an improvement.

**Refused — all 38 PUBLIC_CALIBRATIONS ids.** Zero observations. Nothing to fit. These are scalar
weights inside a linear score, not probabilities with a recorded binary outcome, so no volume of
`calibration_observations` would fit them; they need a different instrument.

## The 9 ranking ids, from traces already on disk

The one offline path to any public id: 226 `local_evidence.rank_features` events in `.scce/traces`
give 87 candidate pools, 64 of them usable against the labelled suite. Fitting the vector on 48
questions and testing on 16 held out:

```
  top-1 on FIT questions      bootstrap 93.8%   fitted 93.8%   (in-sample, not evidence)
  top-1 on HELD-OUT questions bootstrap 100.0%   fitted 100.0%   gain 0.0pp  paired se 0.0pp
  no-improvement on held-out questions; NOT installable either way -- the only labels are the sealed suite's.
```

Bootstrap is already at ceiling on the held-out questions, so there is no headroom to demonstrate.
Two reasons this stays uninstalled regardless: no held-out gain, and the only labels available are
the sealed suite's, so fitting production constants on them would unseal the benchmark.

Note for `tools/fit-ranking-weights.mjs`: it reports top-1 on the same questions it fit on. On this
data that in-sample number (93.8%) is *lower* than held-out (100%), which shows how little the
comparison constrains anything.

## Remaining uncertainty

- **The dataset is live.** Row count moved 6628 -> 6632 during this session; the system is still
  writing observations. Counts here are a snapshot, not a frozen dataset.
- **The runtime sees less data than this tool.** `loadCalibrationModelSet` passes `limit: 5000`
  against 6,632 rows, ordered `created_at DESC`. Production is fitting on the newest 5,000 only,
  and that window will keep narrowing as the table grows.
- **`outcome` for `candidate.mass` is not a graded label.** Its 927 negatives are all
  `final_outcome = "outcome.unknown"`, not a recorded failure. Some of the instability above may
  be that "unknown" is being scored as "wrong".
- **`source_record_id` is assumed to be the right disjointness key.** 908 groups over 3,321 rows;
  if two groups share an underlying document the holdout is less independent than it looks.
- **The ranking holdout is 16 questions.** Enough to show bootstrap is at ceiling, not enough to
  detect a small regression.
- Whether the judge coefficients' consistent +0.04 AUROC translates into better answers is not
  measured here; that needs a live gate, which this task is not permitted to run.
