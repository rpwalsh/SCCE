## What I fixed: (a), and it was never request-specific

### Defect 1 -- the corpus's title never reached the anchors, on any request ever served

`packages/kernel/src/local-evidence-runtime.ts:2759` memoized `sourceEvidenceAnchorsForRequest` by request text
for the life of the process, with no `corpusIdentityGeneration()` guard -- unlike `namedSubjectAnchors`
(`kernel-answer-primitives.ts:14`), which was written with one.

Its first caller in a turn is `compositionDemandTarget` at `production-turn-runtime.ts:1031`. The corpus identity
for that request is primed 43 lines later, at `:1074`. So the anchors every downstream consumer read were derived
against whatever identity set the PREVIOUS turn had primed, and then frozen.

Proven against the built kernel, not inferred:

    identities primed  ->  sourceEvidenceAnchorsForRequest("When did Apollo 11 land on the Moon?")
    {}                 ->  ["did apollo 11 land","moon","apollo land"]
    {apollo 11,apollo} ->  ["apollo 11","apollo land"]
    {} then {apollo 11,apollo}, same process -> ["did apollo 11 land","moon","apollo land"]   <-- the live value

The live trace for that turn shows `turn.corpus_identity identities:["apollo 11","apollo"]` and, three events
later, `turn.source_anchor_audit anchors:["did apollo 11 land","moon","apollo land"]`. The corpus named the right
thing and nothing downstream could see it.

Consequence at the span level, same turn: the pool held both `apollo 11` opening spans and both were REFUSED,
while `apollo` (the Greek god, char 61284), `apollo 8` and `apollo program` were admitted. The community walk cut
10 -> 4 and the opening-block rescue then pulled the Greek god's lead, which is the answer that shipped.

Fix: expire the memo with the corpus identity generation.

### Defect 2 -- 4,790 of 22,224 titles could never be named

`packages/adapters-node/src/postgres.ts` `loadSourceTitles` returned raw `source_title` values while
`sourceIdentityArbitration` tested them for containment inside a request that had ALREADY been reduced to its
units by `IDENTITY_UNIT_SEPARATOR`. A title carrying any separator the request lost could therefore never match.

Measured on the live corpus: 22,224 distinct titles, **4,790 of them (21.6%) unmatchable**. Every
`Star Trek: Deep Space Nine`, `Mercury (planet)`, `Halifax, Nova Scotia`. The live trace confirms it --
"Who created Star Trek: Deep Space Nine?" reports `identities:["star trek"]`, and the whole admitted pool is
`star trek` franchise spans plus `list of star trek: enterprise episodes`, with no DS9 span at all.

Fix: read a title the way a request is read. New `corpusIdentitySurface` in `corpus-identity.ts`; the adapter
normalizes titles once at load, and the two exact-title comparisons
(`evidenceExactSourceAnchorMatches`, `evidenceTitleExactlyMatchesAnchor`) compare the same way, so
`preferExactTitleSources` can see that DS9 is the exact source.

### Defect 3 -- the primary anchor's tie-break could not find its own anchor in the request

`primarySourceAnchorForRequest` ranked by `normalizePriorKey(requestText).indexOf(normalizePriorKey(anchor))`.
`normalizePriorKey` only lowercases, so the request keeps its colon and `indexOf("star trek deep space nine")`
returns -1 -- scored as absent, losing the earlier-is-the-subject tie-break to `star trek`. That is why the live
trace reports `primaryAnchor:"star trek"` on both DS9 questions. Read both sides as units and it resolves.

Also: among the anchors some source is exactly titled with, only the MAXIMAL ones identify. A source titled with
a run contained in a longer titled anchor is a broader subject -- the same rule `corpus-identity.ts` already
applies to a request's own runs, where "moby dick" answers for "moby".

### The four losses are two different things

Two were correct by accident. `alp-country`'s baseline answer is "Australian Labor Party the retrieval political
party in which country. The political parties in the Turkish governmental system..." -- graded correct because it
contains "Australian". `ainu-country`'s was Japanese-government prose about salmon fishing, correct because it
contains "Japanese". The other two are real losses to the evidence-0 defect below.

`alfredgreat-kingdom` went the other way: evidence 0 -> 2, and it now retrieves the Alfred the Great article and
answers "King of the West Saxons" where the gold is "Wessex". A wording miss, not a retrieval miss.

### Evidence-0 is not request-level, and it is not this commit

Eleven factual rows lost all evidence. Asked directly against **current main, which contains 890a8f2**, as turns
1-5 of a warm process, five of those rows return evidence and two return the right answer:

    ev=2  Athens is the capital of which country?    "'Athens' is the capital and largest city of Greece."
    ev=2  Ashoka was an emperor of which ...?        "...the third ruler from the Mauryan dynasty."
    ev=2  The Ainu people are indigenous to ...?
    ev=2  When did Apollo 11 land on the Moon?       (wrong value: answers from Apollo 8)
    ev=1  Who created Star Trek: Deep Space Nine?    (wrong: Enterprise prose)

Anchor derivation is a pure function of the request text. It cannot return evidence 2 here and 0 for the same text
in a 50-row run of the same build. Evidence-0 is sequence- or timing-dependent.

The frozen baseline's own trace, **before this commit**, already carries the mechanism at scale:

    graph.resolve.durable_escalation reached          169 of 323 turns
    refused: escalated=false, deadlineAllowed=false    50 turns
    turns ending at pool_admission admitted=0          17 turns

And the positional gradient exists in the baseline too, over the identical 50 rows in identical order:

    ev0 by decile     1-10  11-20  21-30  31-40  41-50   mean position (uniform = 25.5)
    baseline             0      0      1      1      2       36.8
    L2 build             1      1      4      3      5       31.9

This commit amplifies an existing gradient; it does not create a new failure mode.

### The confounder I could not rule out

`ef9409c` landed between the frozen baseline and this build and rescales graph edge transition weights 50-500x.
`SUBJECT_COMMUNITY_EPSILON = 1e-4` (production-turn-runtime.ts:5440) is an absolute constant compared against
local-push PPR residual mass. Smaller transitions put the residual under epsilon sooner, so
`evidenceInSubjectCommunity` returns a smaller community -- in the baseline trace that walk already cut 24
admitted spans to 1 on `ds9-commander` and 10 to 4 on `apollo-land`. Relation potential was validated on 20
**cloze** rows; cloze is admitted by the near-duplicate path and never reaches that walk, so factual is the only
place this would show and it was never measured there.

### What would settle it

1. Run factual twice in one process with the item order reversed. If evidence-0 follows POSITION rather than row
   id, it is timing, not anchoring.
2. Run factual with `SCCE_TRACE=1` and join `graph.resolve.durable_escalation` with `deadlineAllowed:false` to the
   evidence-0 rows.
3. Ablate `ef9409c` alone on factual.

All three need the server lock. None was available before the authoritative run took it.

### Not measured

`cloze` and `relation` were never run against this build: the lock went to the authoritative 311-row run before I
could take a second hold, and each cloze row costs 35 s so a full cloze pass is 94 minutes. **The scoreboard's
rule -- re-run cloze before claiming any win -- is therefore not satisfied by this lane, and I am not claiming
one.**

Quantified offline instead, reproducing `sourceAnchorRetrievalFeatureGroups`'s own top-5 selection over all 160
cloze prompts against the live title list:

    cloze rows 160:  top-5 retrieval anchors unchanged 125,  at least one displaced 35  (22%)

Two things temper that risk, and one raises it.

- The anchors this commit displaces are mostly prompt scaffolding. Before it, cloze retrieval was anchored on
  `["descendant","complete","sentence","lovelace","countess"]` and `["complete","sentence","intelligence",…]` --
  the instruction words, frozen carry-over from whatever the previous turn primed. Cloze scored 112/160 with
  those, which is evidence that cloze correctness is not very sensitive to this anchor set; admission for a
  near-duplicate request comes from `spanContainsRequestNearDuplicateSentence`, not from anchors.
- Several rows improve visibly: `ada-lovelace-011` goes from `["complete","sentence",…]` to
  `["other acquaintances included","charles wheatstone michael","wheatstone michael faraday"]`, which is the
  cloze sentence itself.
- Against that: incidental single-word titles the corpus happens to hold -- `mouth`, `friends`, `author`,
  `analysis`, and on one row `alan turing` -- now lead, because `corpusNamedRuns` returns identities whenever any
  exist and a long quoted sentence mentions many. On `ada-lovelace-009` a long distinctive phrase is displaced by
  a URL fragment.

If cloze does regress, the principled repair is already named in this file's own doctrine
(`sourceAnchoredEvidenceForRequest`, "anchor heuristics are question-shaped machinery and pick wrong-topic spans
here"): for a request that reproduces a corpus sentence -- `requestSentenceSequences(text).length > 0`, which is
empty for every interrogative -- keep the derived phrase anchors ahead of the identities. I did not add it,
because adding an unmeasured behavioural branch immediately before the authoritative run is worse than reporting
the risk.

## What I proved false

- **"The factual misses are a retrieval problem."** They are not, for most of them. Ten of the twenty-one wrong
  rows retrieve the right article AND rank the answering sentence first; the answer is lost after that.
- **"Identity resolution works and ranking is the problem."** The opposite. `corpus-identity.ts` computes the
  right identity and it never reached a single anchor in production.
- **"`corpusNamedIdentities` needs a better rule."** The pure module was correct throughout. Both defects were
  in its callers: one memo, one normalization mismatch.
