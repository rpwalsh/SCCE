## What I fixed: (a), and it was never request-specific

### Defect 1 -- the corpus's title never reached the anchors, on any request ever served

`packages/kernel/src/local-evidence-runtime.ts:2759` memoized `sourceEvidenceAnchorsForRequest` by request text
for the life of the process, with no `corpusIdentityGeneration()` guard -- unlike `namedSubjectAnchors`
(`kernel-answer-primitives.ts:14`), which was written with one.

Its first caller in a turn is `compositionDemandTarget` at `production-turn-runtime.ts:1031`. The corpus identity
for that request is primed 43 lines later, at `:1074`. So the anchors every downstream consumer read were derived
against whatever identity set the PREVIOUS turn had primed, and then frozen.

Proven against the built kernel, not inferred:

    identities primed  ->  sourceEvidenceAnchorsForRequest("When did Apollo 11 land on the Moon?")
    {}                 ->  ["did apollo 11 land","moon","apollo land"]
    {apollo 11,apollo} ->  ["apollo 11","apollo land"]
    {} then {apollo 11,apollo}, same process -> ["did apollo 11 land","moon","apollo land"]   <-- the live value

The live trace for that turn shows `turn.corpus_identity identities:["apollo 11","apollo"]` and, three events
later, `turn.source_anchor_audit anchors:["did apollo 11 land","moon","apollo land"]`. The corpus named the right
thing and nothing downstream could see it.

Consequence at the span level, same turn: the pool held both `apollo 11` opening spans and both were REFUSED,
while `apollo` (the Greek god, char 61284), `apollo 8` and `apollo program` were admitted. The community walk cut
10 -> 4 and the opening-block rescue then pulled the Greek god's lead, which is the answer that shipped.

Fix: expire the memo with the corpus identity generation.

### Defect 2 -- 4,790 of 22,224 titles could never be named

`packages/adapters-node/src/postgres.ts` `loadSourceTitles` returned raw `source_title` values while
`sourceIdentityArbitration` tested them for containment inside a request that had ALREADY been reduced to its
units by `IDENTITY_UNIT_SEPARATOR`. A title carrying any separator the request lost could therefore never match.

Measured on the live corpus: 22,224 distinct titles, **4,790 of them (21.6%) unmatchable**. Every
`Star Trek: Deep Space Nine`, `Mercury (planet)`, `Halifax, Nova Scotia`. The live trace confirms it --
"Who created Star Trek: Deep Space Nine?" reports `identities:["star trek"]`, and the whole admitted pool is
`star trek` franchise spans plus `list of star trek: enterprise episodes`, with no DS9 span at all.

Fix: read a title the way a request is read. New `corpusIdentitySurface` in `corpus-identity.ts`; the adapter
normalizes titles once at load, and the two exact-title comparisons
(`evidenceExactSourceAnchorMatches`, `evidenceTitleExactlyMatchesAnchor`) compare the same way, so
`preferExactTitleSources` can see that DS9 is the exact source.

### Defect 3 -- the primary anchor's tie-break could not find its own anchor in the request

`primarySourceAnchorForRequest` ranked by `normalizePriorKey(requestText).indexOf(normalizePriorKey(anchor))`.
`normalizePriorKey` only lowercases, so the request keeps its colon and `indexOf("star trek deep space nine")`
returns -1 -- scored as absent, losing the earlier-is-the-subject tie-break to `star trek`. That is why the live
trace reports `primaryAnchor:"star trek"` on both DS9 questions. Read both sides as units and it resolves.

Also: among the anchors some source is exactly titled with, only the MAXIMAL ones identify. A source titled with
a run contained in a longer titled anchor is a broader subject -- the same rule `corpus-identity.ts` already
applies to a request's own runs, where "moby dick" answers for "moby".

## What I proved false

- **"The factual misses are a retrieval problem."** They are not, for most of them. Ten of the twenty-one wrong
  rows retrieve the right article AND rank the answering sentence first; the answer is lost after that.
- **"Identity resolution works and ranking is the problem."** The opposite. `corpus-identity.ts` computes the
  right identity and it never reached a single anchor in production.
- **"`corpusNamedIdentities` needs a better rule."** The pure module was correct throughout. Both defects were
  in its callers: one memo, one normalization mismatch.
