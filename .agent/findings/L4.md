## 3. Defect A -- the ranker has no answerhood signal

`turnProofEvidenceLimit` is 2 (`kernel.ts:169`). So of 24 admitted spans, `evidenceForRequest`
(`local-evidence-runtime.ts:123`) keeps 16 and the **top two decide the answer**.

`evidenceForRequest` ranks by lexical overlap plus alignment boosts. For an encyclopedia article that works: the
source is short and is *about* one subject. For a book it cannot work: "Pequod" is on 360 spans and "captain" on
hundreds, and nothing in the ranker asks which span contains a sentence that binds them. What it selected for
"Who is the captain of the Pequod in Moby-Dick?" was `char_start=176542`:

    "Good again. Now then, thou not only wantest to go a-whaling ... Captain Peleg started me on the errand."

The mouth then correctly refused to speak, because no sentence in it answers. The answering sentences exist and
were never ranked -- 38 sentences of Moby Dick carry "Pequod" and "captain" together.

**The predicate the mouth requires before it will speak was never used to decide what the mouth would see.**
`answerCoversRequest(..., { relationRequired: true })` is applied as a veto at `mouth.ts:1499` and as the window
gate at `mouth.ts:7966`; it is applied nowhere in ranking.

### Fix: `b15d8fd`, `6da3861`

`spanCarriesAnsweringSentence` orders the pool ahead of relevance: a span leads when one of its own sentences
names the request's subject and carries every relation unit asked about. It is **silent by construction** -- when
no span passes, or all do, the term is equal for every pair and the order is exactly what it was. No weight was
added to the linear score, no constant introduced; the one number is a commented cost bound on characters scanned.
`local_evidence.answerhood_order` reports `active` / `bypassed_not_applicable`, how many spans it admitted, and
whether it actually moved the lead, so an inert pass cannot look like a deciding one.

Threaded through both production call sites with the turn's learned closed class (`requestClosedClassWords()`),
which is what tells the relation from the request's scaffolding. Without it, `coverageUnits` is empty and the pass
is a no-op -- so the cloze lane, whose prompt is a near-duplicate of a source sentence and never leaves a
non-empty missing-relation set, is untouched by construction.

**Scoped to the population it was built for (`4c5e2bc`).** The ordering applies only to spans whose source carries
an identity beyond its title. An article's identity IS its title, so the title and opening-block priors already
encode which chunk answers and a deeper chunk that merely repeats the relation word must not displace the lead --
"Which country are the Ainu from?" has a lead that never says "country", and the opening-block pin does not rescue
it either, because `openingSpanAnswersRequest` wants exactly the content unit the lead lacks. That is the
correct-to-declined shape the swarm saw on the factual workload, and this ordering is now inert on a pool of
articles (21,915 of 23,421 sources), asserted in the test. It also means the 187 ms scan does not run on
article-only pools at all, which is nearly every factual and cloze turn.

Regression check: `packages/kernel/src/__tests__/evidence-answerhood-ranking.test.ts`. It asserts the defect (the
old relevance order) and the fix in the same test, so removing the sort term fails it.

Cost, measured on a real 24-span Moby-Dick pool (3,505 chars mean): **187 ms cold, 50 ms warm** for the whole
pass, 7.8 ms per span, dominated by tokenising the span once. The per-sentence scan only runs on spans the
whole-span test admits, and that test is a necessary condition for any sentence of the span to pass, so on a pool
where nothing answers the scan never runs at all. The second `evidenceForRequest` call of a turn sees a pool of 2
and is free. Against turns currently running 8-31 s this is inside the noise, but it is a real addition and it is
on every factual turn, not only book ones.

## 4. Defect B -- structural residue answering

Three answers in the baseline were a source's apparatus. Each has a named producer:

| row | answer | producer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| harker | Project Gutenberg licence header | `mouth.contradiction_fallback`, production-turn-runtime.ts:4340 |
| hispaniola | chapter table of contents | `mouth.source_summary_fallback`, production-turn-runtime.ts:4392 |
| toto | unrelated interior prose | `mouth.contradiction_fallback` |

Both lanes are answers of last resort that read an admitted span when every narrower lane produced nothing, and
neither asked whether the span was the source's front matter. Reproduced offline: `summarizeAdmittedSource` run on
the `char_start = 0` span of Dracula returns its chapter index, and on Moby Dick returns "or, THE WHALE. Knights
and Squires. The Whiteness of the Whale...". The centrality measure is working as designed -- a table of contents
is maximally self-similar, so it wins.

### Fix: `d169998` (the predicate itself landed inside another lane's commit `e084da2`, see section 6)

`spanIsSourceFrontMatter(span)` = `charStart === 0 && evidenceIdentityBeyondTitle(span)`. Both halves already
existed and the opening-block pin already refuses exactly this span (`production-turn-runtime.ts:1808`); the two
last-resort lanes asked neither. No phrase, no lane name, no markup dialect, no casing.

Measured on the live corpus, `char_start = 0` promoted spans by ingestion lane:

    lane          opening spans   identity distinct from title
    wikipedia         21,915           0
    oss_code             425         425
    oss_docs             418         418
    (null lane)        1,054         971
    gutenberg             55          30      (all nine benchmark books among them)

So the predicate fires on exactly the lanes whose opening block is apparatus and **cannot reach an article's
definitional lead**, which is what the capitals gate depends on. Spot-checked what the null lane's 971 are: the
repository's own TypeScript files, whose `char_start = 0` span is the import block. Front matter there too.

`aca6bd9` extends the same rule to the ranking pass: front matter never takes the answerhood lead either. Treasure
Island's chapter index was taking it, because the titles it lists carry "treasure island" and "ship" between them.
Confirmed to be the deciding factor by running the same two spans with and without an identity: with it the
binding sentence leads, without it the index does.

### It is not redundant with `structural-residue.ts`

Another lane shipped a surface measure (symbol density times one minus bigram diversity, Otsu-cut over the
corpus's 962,249 sentences). Measured against the exact strings the baseline answered with:

    surface                                          residue score   verdict
    Gutenberg licence header (harker's answer)           0.0050      admitted
    "NARRATIVE CONTINUED BY THE DOCTOR..." x3             0.0627      admitted
    Moby Dick chapter index                              0.0429      admitted
    Dracula chapter index                                0.1525      REFUSED
    real Moby Dick prose (control)                       0.0000      admitted

Three of four book apparatus surfaces are inside prose by that measure, because a licence header **is** fluent
prose. The two mechanisms are complementary: one measures the shape of the surface, the other asks where the
surface sits in a source that has an identity of its own. `source-front-matter.test.ts` asserts that gap so a
future merge of the two cannot silently drop this material.

## 5. What I proved false

- **"The four evidence-0 rows are a relation-channel failure."** Three of four admitted 24 spans of the right book
  and attached a proof. Section 2.
- **"The books produce no relations because they predate the prose relation channel."** They produce none because
  the Gutenberg lane never ran it; 21,909 Wikipedia versions ingested over the same period manage 1.4%. L5's
  measurement, quoted with its permission.
- **"Boilerplate rejection is already covered by the structural-residue measure."** Measured: it admits the
  Gutenberg licence header at 0.005 and the Treasure Island running head at 0.063. Section 4.
- **"An offline ranking harness can stand in for the live turn."** It cannot, and the reason is specific: with no
  corpus signal primed, `namedSubjectAnchors` returns the whole request as one run, every content unit becomes a
  subject unit, the relation set empties and `answerCoversRequest` returns true for everything. Every offline
  number in this file was taken with the corpus signals primed to the anchors the live trace recorded.
- **"The 4,000-character slice in `spanContainsRequestNearDuplicateSentence` is why the book rows return
  nothing."** Three independent measurements say no. `requestSentenceSequences` returns 0 sequences for every
  book question (1 for a cloze prompt), so the function returns false on its first line and never reaches the
  slice. Of 2,583 promoted book spans, 0 exceed 6,000 characters, so the 4,000 branch is unreachable for books
  anyway. And of the 196 sentences in those books carrying a benchmark subject with its relation, 196 sit before
  character 4,000 and none at or past it.

## 5a. The caps that ARE real, and why I left them

Three admission-path slices decide what is reachable rather than how fast it is reached:
`evidenceContentAnchorFitsRequest` (:2752), `spanIsAboutAnchor` (:2769), `evidenceContentMentionsAnchor` (:2794),
all at 4,000 characters. Measured corpus-wide: **38,789 of 73,480 promoted spans exceed 4,000 characters** (53%),
mean hidden tail **969 characters**. By the rule as posted they are modelling parameters and belong in
`calibrations/`. The same file already carries the comment "slice(0,4000) blinded the gate to the last ~90 chars
of a 4096-byte chunk" at :2717 -- the defect was found once and fixed in one place out of four.

I did not remove them. They hide nothing that answers a book question (measured above), they sit on L1's and L2's
seam, and changing what 53% of the corpus admits shortly before the authoritative run would invalidate every
before/after recorded tonight, including the ones already taken.

### Why 8 is not reachable by selection, measured

The three rows I cannot reach have no span that could be selected, because the book has no sentence that carries
the question. Counted over the full text of each book:

    "Who is Sherlock Holmes's companion?"

Cloze is the asset and it is safe: **0 of 40 regressed across both runs**, and the answers are now short and

**I predicted kansas would flip and it did not. The trace says why, and it is neither ranking nor my guard.**

    Dorothy live    answerhood pool=24 answering=0  bypassed  units=["does","dorothy","live","wonderful","wizard"]
    Dorothy's dog   answerhood pool=24 answering=18 ACTIVE    lead unchanged
    Treasure Island answerhood pool=24 answering=0  bypassed  -- summary lane withheld, reason
                    "every-identity-bound-span-is-the-source's-front-matter"

The coverage units for "Where does Dorothy live in The Wonderful Wizard of Oz?" include **"does"**. It is the
request's auxiliary, it is four characters so the length filter keeps it, and the turn's learned closed class does
not contain it -- `requestClosedClassWords` only intersects the corpus closed class with the request's first two
words, and the corpus closed class did not carry "does" at this hydration. So the relation set the answer must
satisfy contains a word no sentence of a novel will ever contain, and `answering` is 0 for all 24 spans. The
char-0 Oz span that carries "Dorothy lived in the midst of the great Kansas prairies" is not in the admitted 24
either, so neither the pin nor the ranker could have reached it.

That is a closed-class derivation gap on the scaffolding seam, not a ranking or admission defect, and it is not
mine to change at this hour. It is the single highest-value follow-up for this workload: one request word wrongly
counted as content makes the answerhood gate unsatisfiable for the whole turn.

`toto` shows the other failure mode honestly: the ordering ran, reported `active`, found 18 of 24 spans answering
and did not move the lead. When most of the pool passes, the test carries no information -- which is what the
silent-when-it-does-not-discriminate design is for, and the trace says so rather than implying work was done.
