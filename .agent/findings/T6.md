## The defect

`requestUnitMatchesSurface` and `requestUnitSharesStem` each accepted a one-letter extension only when that
letter was literally `"s"`. That is an English plural rule, and it is why "What did he discover?" could not read
the answering sentence's "discovery" — the extra letter is `y`, so the match was refused before similarity ever
ran. Both rules also carried the corpus's real regression cases: "capita" is not "capital", "Borna" is not "born".

## The signal used, and why it separates

**The Kneser-Ney continuation count at order 1: how many distinct words the corpus puts in front of a surface.**
This is the same property `deriveClosedClassWords` ranks by, and the property KN itself substitutes for frequency
— "Francisco" is frequent and continues one context.

A free form is preceded by many different words. A fragment of a fixed phrase is preceded by one. On the live
brain's whole 2,012-model corpus, "capita" occurs 119 times after **2** distinct words ("per" is 95% of them),
while "capital" occurs 1,428 times after **295**. Ranking all 3,925 forms of the one-letter-pair vocabulary by
this measure, the most extreme are exactly the phrase fragments — `lankan, fro, capita, aires, rican, thirds,
facto, rica, vegas, indies, lanka, offs` — and "capita" is 3rd.

Diversity alone is frequency-dependent, so the cut is not a number. The corpus's own type/token relation is
fitted over its whole vocabulary and a form is *bound* when its residual is further into the lower tail than
chance explains across a vocabulary of that size — fewer than one such form expected. The tail is bounded exactly
by Mills' inequality (`P(Z<z) <= phi(z)/|z|`), so there is no approximation and no tuned constant. A form with too
few tokens for even a single context to reach that tail has told the corpus nothing and is *unknown*, not *free*:
that is what refuses "Borna" for "born", and an unseen surface, which is what refuses "Majorian" for "Bajoran".

Fit on the 6 models a live turn holds resident: `log k = -0.129 + 0.832 log t`, sd 0.421, over 17,364 forms.

## Remaining uncertainty

1. **find/found is not closed.** It is not a prefix pair, so this mechanism never sees it; the similarity floor
   scores it 0.6 against 0.72. It cannot be separated by the evidence available: find/found and mind/mound have
   *identical* orthographic profiles (shared prefix 1, edit distance 2, lengths 4/5), and across the ind→ound
   family the distributional ranking is wrong — grind/ground (a real pair) scores 0.0029 on PPMI cosine, below
   mind/mound (not a pair) at 0.0115. G4's narrative half stays open. A threshold that admits find/found (0.448
   ovlR) also rejects the real bind/bound and wind/wound, so it would be fitted to one example.
2. **This widens matching inside the one-letter population.** Where the corpus attests both forms freely, pairs
   the literal rule refused are now accepted: war/ward, sea/seal, idea/ideal, mode/model, count/county. This is
   the price of removing the English rule, and it is bounded by the boundness test — lanka/lankan and
   capita/capital stay refused. Only a live gate can price it; that has not been run.
3. **A form with few tokens is refused, not accepted.** deliver/delivery is a true pair the resident 6 models
   cannot support (deliver t=6, no power). Missed matches, never wrong ones. More resident models would fix it.
4. **`capita`/`capitals`** — two letters apart — is still accepted by the general prefix-ratio rule (6/8 = 0.75 >=
   0.72). The boundness test would refuse it if applied to the whole prefix path, which is a narrowing this task
   did not take.
5. **majorian/bajoran** is still accepted by `requestUnitSharesStem` through the similarity floor, unchanged by
   this task. The oracle refuses it; the floor does not. `answerCoversRequest`'s `subjectMatches` is what guards
   it live.
6. **Cost:** 0.9–1.5 s to fit 17,364 forms from 2.29M gram keys, once per distinct model set, deferred to the
   first question asked of it. Measured 7.6 s before the entries/regex memoisation.
